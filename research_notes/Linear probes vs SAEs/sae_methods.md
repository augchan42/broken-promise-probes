# Sparse autoencoders and related dictionary-learning methods for LLM interpretability (state as of November 2026)

Scope note: Qwen-Scope and SAE-vs-probe benchmarks are covered by other researchers and are only mentioned in passing here.

Source-verification note: items marked [V] were fetched or read in this research session (Nov 2026). Unmarked items are well-known papers cited from prior knowledge with their canonical arXiv/transformer-circuits URLs; the IDs and headline claims are standard, but I did not re-open them this session, so the report writer should treat exact numbers in unmarked items as "check before quoting."

## Architectures and training

### Takeaway
The field moved from ReLU+L1 SAEs (Anthropic 2023-24) to activation functions that separate "which latents fire" from "how large" (TopK, JumpReLU, BatchTopK), and by 2025-26 to Matryoshka (nested-prefix) training plus targeted penalties on high-frequency latents and optional KL / end-to-end finetuning; Gemma Scope 2 (Sept 2025 paper, released Dec 2025/Jan 2026) is the reference recipe: JumpReLU + quadratic L0 penalty + Matryoshka + untied encoder/decoder + chat-rollout finetuning for IT models [V].

### Cited Findings
**Basic setup**
- An SAE learns f(x) = sigma(W_enc x + b_enc), x_hat = W_dec f(x) + b_dec, with f non-negative and sparse (far fewer than n non-zero entries); Gemma Scope 2 calls the learned directions "latents" to distinguish them from hypothesized true "features" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper (McDougall, Conmy, Kramár, Lieberum, Rajamanoharan, Nanda; dated 2025-09-16)](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- ReLU + L1 penalty was the original recipe (Bricken et al. 2023 "Towards Monosemanticity", 1-layer transformer; Cunningham et al. 2023, arXiv 2309.08600) [V, as described in the GS2 paper] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf); [Towards Monosemanticity (Oct 2023)](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features); [Cunningham et al. arXiv 2309.08600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08600)
- "Scaling Monosemanticity" (May 2024) scaled ReLU+L1 SAEs to Claude 3 Sonnet's middle residual stream at 1M, 4M and 34M latents and reported abstract, multilingual, multimodal features (e.g. Golden Gate Bridge, code-security, deception-related features) — [Templeton et al. 2024](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/)
- TopK SAEs (Gao et al., OpenAI, arXiv 2406.04093, June 2024) zero all but the top K pre-activations, removing the L1 shrinkage problem and allowing direct L0 control; they trained a 16M-latent SAE on GPT-4 activations and reported clean scaling laws in width and k; they introduced the "normalized MSE" (FVU) metric used widely since — [Gao et al. arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093); FVU naming confirmed in [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf) [V]
- JumpReLU (Rajamanoharan et al., GDM, arXiv 2407.14435, July 2024): sigma(z) = z ⊙ H(z − theta) with a learnable per-latent threshold theta > 0; trained with an L0 penalty via straight-through estimators; "Both TopK and JumpReLU SAEs allow for greater separation between the tasks of determining which latents are active, and estimating their magnitudes" [V for formula/quote] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf); [Rajamanoharan et al. arXiv 2407.14435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14435)
- Gated SAEs (Rajamanoharan et al., GDM, arXiv 2404.16014, April 2024) are the predecessor of JumpReLU: a gating path decides which latents fire and a separate magnitude path estimates size, to avoid L1 shrinkage — [arXiv 2404.16014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16014)
- p-annealing (Karvonen et al. 2024, in "Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for LM Interpretability with Board Game Models", arXiv 2408.00113) anneals the sparsity penalty from L1 toward an Lp (p<1) penalty during training — [arXiv 2408.00113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00113)
- BatchTopK (Bussmann, Leask, Nanda; NeurIPS 2024 workshop, arXiv 2412.06410) "relax[es] the top-k constraint to the batch-level, allowing for a variable number of latents to be active per sample" [V] — [BatchTopK, mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2024/bussmann2024neuripsw-batchtopk); [arXiv 2412.06410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06410)
- Matryoshka SAEs (Bussmann, Nabeshima, Karvonen, Nanda, arXiv 2503.17547, March 2025; earlier LessWrong posts Dec 2024) train nested prefixes of the dictionary to reconstruct independently so early latents learn general concepts and later ones specifics; used BatchTopK as activation on Gemma-2-2B and reported reduced feature absorption [V] — [arXiv 2503.17547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17547v1); [LessWrong: Learning Multi-Level Features with Matryoshka SAEs](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rKM9b6B2LqwSB5ToN/learning-multi-level-features-with-matryoshka-saes). A 2026 secondary survey states first-letter absorption on Gemma-2-2B fell from 0.49 to 0.05 at matched sparsity with a small explained-variance cost [V, secondary] — [Feature Superposition in Neural Networks: From Theory to Practice, arXiv 2609.06862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06862)
- Gemma Scope 2 (GDM) uses JumpReLU with a quadratic L0 penalty (targets a specific L0 rather than linear L0), plus a secondary penalty that specifically targets high-frequency latents; with an aggressive version "we can entirely eliminate latents with frequency greater than 10%"; Matryoshka training was used for the SAEs [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Gemma Scope 2 hyperparameters: Adam with (beta1, beta2) = (0, 0.999), lr 7e-5 with cosine warmup over 1,000 steps, batch size 4,096, JumpReLU bandwidth 0.001, sparsity coefficient linearly warmed up over 50,000 steps; activations normalized by a fixed scalar to unit mean squared norm so hyperparameters transfer across layers/sites; pre-encoder bias during training folded in afterwards; decoder columns kept unit-norm with gradient components parallel to them projected out (Bricken et al. 2023 trick) [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- End-to-end / KL finetuning: GS2 finetunes selected SAEs and transcoders with L = (MSE + alpha·beta·KL(p(x), p(x_hat)))/(1+beta), alpha = MSE/(KL+1e-8) held constant w.r.t. gradients, following Braun et al. 2024 (e2e SAEs, arXiv 2405.12241) and Karvonen 2025, "with the goal of learning latents which are functionally important for the model's predictions rather than just for reconstructing activations" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf); [Braun et al. arXiv 2405.12241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12241)

**Why encoder and decoder are untied**
- GS2: "W_enc is initialized as the transpose of W_dec, but they are not tied afterwards" citing Conerly et al. 2024 (Anthropic training update) and Gao et al. 2024 [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf). The standard rationale: the decoder direction is the feature's write-direction in activation space, while the encoder must also suppress interference from other non-orthogonal (superposed) features, so the best detector is not the same vector as the best reconstruction direction (Bricken et al. 2023 discussion) — [Towards Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features)
- Feature absorption is a concrete case: when a parent latent is absorbed, its encoder direction develops "holes" for child tokens while the decoder stays similar, which only an untied encoder can do (Chanin et al.) — [A is for Absorption, arXiv 2409.14507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14507)

**Choosing width and L0**
- Gemma Scope 2 releases widths 16k, 64k, 256k, 1M and recommends 64k or 256k; L0 buckets are "small" (10-20), "medium" (30-60, recommended default), "large" (60-150) [V] — [HF model card google/gemma-scope-2-4b-it](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope-2-4b-it)
- GS2 evaluates by sweeping target L0 at fixed width and plotting delta LM loss (CE increase when spliced in) and FVU vs L0; "Higher L0s (and wider SAEs) lead to better performance"; delta loss is "consistently higher for residual stream SAEs compared to MLP and attention SAEs, whereas FVU is roughly comparable across sites" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- GS2 auto-interp finds "lower-frequency latents tend to be more interpretable"; higher-frequency latents slightly less so [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- A June 2026 vision-SAE evaluation paper finds increased overcompleteness can reduce perturbation alignment with annotated concepts, i.e. wider is not automatically more interpretable [V, abstract only] — [arXiv 2606.24716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24716)
- SAEBench (Karvonen et al., arXiv 2503.09532, March 2025) is the standard multi-metric benchmark (absorption, sparse probing, spurious-correlation removal, targeted probe perturbation, autointerp, unlearning) and reports that proxy metrics (FVU/L0) do not reliably predict practical performance — [arXiv 2503.09532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09532)

**Scaling laws**
- Gao et al. fit power laws of reconstruction MSE in number of latents and k, and joint laws with compute; they report that larger SAEs need more tokens and that the TopK form scales cleanly up to 16M latents — [arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093); Anthropic reported similar compute-optimal scaling for ReLU SAEs — [Scaling Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/)

**Compute engineering for multi-layer dictionaries**
- Multi-layer models: batch size scales O(layers), naive FLOPs and parameter counts O(layers^2) because the decoder is dense over layer pairs; GS2 used a variant of Leo Gao's sparse kernels to get ~O(layers) effective FLOPs and extreme decoder sharding [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)

**2026 architecture variants (preprints, not yet established)**
- Subspace-Aware SAEs (June 2026) argue the one-direction-per-feature assumption causes splitting; report reduced splitting/absorption on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B [V, abstract via search] — [arXiv 2606.06333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)
- "Size Doesn't Matter: Cosine-Scored Sparse Autoencoders" (June 2026) [V, title only] — [arXiv 2606.15054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15054)
- Supervised SAEs for compositional representations (Feb 2026) [V, title only] — [arXiv 2602.00924](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00924)
- SAE application spread in 2026: diffusion LMs (DLM-Scope, arXiv 2602.05859), Whisper encoders (arXiv 2605.12225), vision-language-action robot models (arXiv 2603.19183) [V, abstracts via search] — [DLM-Scope](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05859); [Whisper SAEs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12225); [VLA SAEs](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.19183)

### Inferences
- By 2026 the default "good SAE" recipe is: JumpReLU or BatchTopK, Matryoshka nesting, explicit frequency penalty, untied weights, optional KL finetune. Plain ReLU+L1 is now mostly historical, but it remains relevant because of its better seed stability (see failure modes).
- L0 is chosen by convention (tens to ~150 for residual SAEs) and by downstream trade-off plots, not by a principled criterion; width is chosen by compute budget and use case.

### Gaps
- I did not find a single 2025-26 paper giving a principled rule for choosing L0 (some 2025 LessWrong work argues "true L0" can be estimated and that too-low L0 causes feature mixing; not verified this session).
- Exact scaling-law exponents from Gao et al. not re-checked.

## Failure modes and limits

### Takeaway
SAEs reconstruct imperfectly (residual "dark matter" that hurts downstream loss when spliced in), their latents are not canonical (splitting, absorption, seed dependence, meta-SAE decomposability), they can emit high-frequency/dense and dead latents, and their outputs depend on training data distribution (base vs chat); 2025-26 fixes (Matryoshka, frequency penalties, chat finetuning, BatchTopK crosscoders) reduce but do not remove these problems.

### Cited Findings
- Feature splitting: wider SAEs break a general latent into finer ones (Bricken et al. 2023); GS2 lists "Better understanding the phenomenon of 'feature splitting' ... Do Matryoshka SAEs help resolve this?" as an open question (Sept 2025) [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Feature absorption (Chanin et al., "A is for Absorption", arXiv 2409.14507, Sept 2024): a seemingly monosemantic parent latent (e.g. "starts with S") fails to fire on some tokens because a more specific child latent (e.g. "short") absorbs its direction; sparsity pressure causes this whenever features are hierarchical; varying width/sparsity does not fix it — [arXiv 2409.14507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14507). Matryoshka reduces it (see above) [V] — [arXiv 2503.17547](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17547v1)
- Non-canonicity: Leask, Bussmann, Nanda et al. (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2502.04878) show with SAE stitching that smaller SAEs are incomplete (larger SAEs contain "novel" latents) and with meta-SAEs that latents are not atomic: an "Einstein" latent decomposes into "scientist", "Germany", "famous person"; they conclude alternatives should be explored [V] — [arXiv 2502.04878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04878)
- Seed instability: Paulo & Belrose (EleutherAI, arXiv 2501.16615, Jan 2025): on a 131K-latent SAE on a Llama 3 8B MLP, "only 30% of the features were shared across different seeds"; overlap decreases as latent count grows; ReLU+L1 SAEs are more seed-stable than TopK, even controlling for sparsity; authors say the effect is not mainly due to absorption [V] — [arXiv 2501.16615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16615)
- Dark matter: Engels, Riggs, Tegmark (arXiv 2410.14670, Oct 2024) find that a large fraction of SAE error is linearly predictable from the input activation (so larger SAEs could capture it) but a remainder is not, and this "nonlinear" error does not shrink much with SAE scale — [arXiv 2410.14670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14670). GS2 lists "Can we find the 'dark matter' of truly non-linear features?" as open (Sept 2025) [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Downstream loss: splicing SAE reconstructions in raises CE loss; GS2's delta-loss metric shows residual SAEs are worst; FVU "is purely a measure of the SAE's ability to reconstruct the input activations, not taking into account the causal effect of any error on the downstream loss" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf). Gao et al. reported that splicing their 16M GPT-4 SAE yields loss comparable to a model trained with roughly 10x less compute — [arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093)
- Dense / high-frequency latents: GDM (March 2025) attributes latents firing on >~10% of tokens to the L0 penalty in JumpReLU and proposes a quadratic-frequency penalty that largely removes them at small reconstruction cost [V] — [GDM, Negative Results for SAEs on Downstream Tasks (AF, 26 Mar 2025)](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks). Dead latents were a major problem in early ReLU SAEs; TopK with AuxK loss (Gao et al.) and resampling reduce them — [arXiv 2406.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093)
- Interpretability-score inflation: GDM argues standard autointerp averages uniformly across latents and should be frequency-weighted, because removing one frequent uninterpretable latent matters more than a rare one [V] — [GDM AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks). Anthropic's May 2026 circuits update shows two CLT features with near-identical top activations and green-related top unembeds that "have different causal effects on model behavior", i.e. activation-based labels can mislead about function [V] — [Transformer Circuits May 2026 Update](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/may-update/index.html). EleutherAI's automated interpretation pipeline (Paulo et al., arXiv 2410.13928) introduced cheaper scoring (detection, fuzzing, intervention) and noted explanation scores vary with method — [arXiv 2410.13928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13928)
- Spurious composition: GS2 asks whether SAEs "learn spurious compositions of independent features to improve sparsity as has been shown to happen in toy models (Anders et al., 2024)" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Dataset artifacts / distribution sensitivity: GemmaScope (v1) SAEs were trained on pretraining text, not chat rollouts; GDM found retraining or finetuning on chat data improved OOD harmful-intent probing, with finetuning closing about half the gap to dense probes [V] — [GDM AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks). GS2 therefore initializes IT SAEs from PT SAEs and finetunes on rollouts generated from OpenAssistant/oasst1 and LMSYS-Chat-1M prompts, citing Kissane et al. 2024 that PT-to-IT transfer "typically does not require resampling a large fraction of latents" [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf); [Kissane et al., "SAEs (usually) transfer between base and chat models" (LessWrong, 2024)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/fmwk6qxrpW8d4jvbd/saes-usually-transfer-between-base-and-chat-models)
- Dataset debugging as a positive side: GDM found SAE latents exposed a spurious correlation (a question-mark latent separating harmful vs harmless datasets) [V] — [GDM AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)
- Crosscoder-specific artifacts: L1 crosscoders mislabel latents as chat-only via "Complete Shrinkage" (base decoder norm pushed to zero) and "Latent Decoupling"; most L1 "chat-only" latents are not truly chat-specific, while most BatchTopK ones are (Minder, Dumas, Juang, Chughtai, Nanda, arXiv 2504.02922, Apr 2025) [V] — [arXiv 2504.02922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02922)

### Inferences
- Seed instability plus non-canonicity means a primer should present SAE latents as one useful basis among many, not as "the model's features"; claims that depend on a specific latent should be checked across seeds/widths.
- There is a stability/quality trade-off: TopK-family SAEs score better on reconstruction but are less seed-stable than ReLU+L1 (Paulo & Belrose).

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 paper that resolves dark matter or seed instability; the GS2 "open problems" list (Sept 2025) still lists both.
- A 2026 title "Falsifying sparse autoencoder reasoning features in language models" appeared in a reference list but I could not locate the paper; not citable.
- Exact Engels et al. percentages (fraction of error linearly predictable) not re-verified.

## Successor and related methods

### Takeaway
The center of mass shifted from single-site SAEs toward dictionaries that model computation: transcoders and skip transcoders (MLP input → output), crosscoders (multi-layer or multi-model), cross-layer transcoders with attribution graphs (Anthropic 2025, now open-sourced via circuit-tracer + Neuronpedia and shipped by GDM in Gemma Scope 2), and weight-space methods (APD → SPD, Goodfire/Apollo, 2025) that decompose parameters instead of activations.

### Cited Findings
- Transcoders (Dunefsky, Chlenski, Nanda, arXiv 2406.11944, June 2024) are trained to approximate an MLP's output from its input, giving input-invariant weight-based circuit analysis; GS2 describes them the same way [V for definition] — [arXiv 2406.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11944); [Gemma Scope paper arXiv 2408.05147](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05147)
- Skip transcoders (Paulo, Shabalin, Belrose, arXiv 2501.18823, Jan 2025) add an affine skip connection; reported lower reconstruction loss at no interpretability cost — [arXiv 2501.18823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18823). GS2 confirms affine skips improve FVU-vs-L0 for transcoders and CLTs and give sparser attribution graphs [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Crosscoders (Lindsey et al., Anthropic, Oct 2024) read/write concatenated activations from multiple layers or multiple models; enable model diffing (shared vs model-exclusive latents) — [Sparse Crosscoders](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/crosscoders/index.html). GS2 ships "weakly causal" partial crosscoders on 4 residual layers (25/50/65/85% depth), widths 64k-1M, L0 50 or 150, initialized by concatenating de-duplicated Matryoshka prefixes from single-layer SAEs [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Model-diffing crosscoders base vs chat: BatchTopK crosscoders on Gemma-2-2b base/chat find genuinely chat-specific, interpretable latents; "Latent Scaling" detects spurious chat-only latents [V] — [arXiv 2504.02922](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02922). A follow-up LessWrong post reports diff-SAEs / chat-SAEs found more chat-specific latents than crosscoders [V, via search summary] — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xmpauEXEerzYcJKNm/)
- Cross-layer transcoders and attribution graphs: Anthropic's "Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models" (Ameisen, Lindsey et al., March 2025) builds a "replacement model" from CLTs (each latent reads from one layer's residual stream and writes to all later MLP outputs), adds error nodes, freezes attention, and computes linear attribution graphs per prompt, then prunes them — [Circuit Tracing methods](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html). The companion "On the Biology of a Large Language Model" (Lindsey et al., March 2025) applies it to Claude 3.5 Haiku: multi-hop reasoning, planning of rhyme words ahead of time, multilingual shared features, arithmetic, hallucination/refusal circuits, unfaithful CoT — [Biology](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)
- GS2 replicates Anthropic's finding that CLT attribution graphs are sparser (fewer nodes/edges to reach a given fraction of influence) than graphs from per-layer transcoders [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Open tooling: Anthropic open-sourced circuit-tracer on 29 May 2025 (built by Anthropic Fellows Michael Hanna and Mateusz Piotrowski; Neuronpedia integration by Decode Research), supporting attribution graphs on Gemma-2-2b (using GemmaScope transcoders) and Llama-3.2-1b, with a Neuronpedia frontend for annotation and interventions [V] — [Anthropic: Open-sourcing circuit tracing tools](https://anthropic.com/research/open-source-circuit-tracing)
- CLT-Forge (March 2026) is a library for training CLTs and building attribution graphs at scale [V, title/abstract] — [arXiv 2603.21014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21014). 2026 CLT applications include protein LMs (ProtoMech, arXiv 2602.12026) and refusal-feature selection (CRaFT, arXiv 2604.01604) [V, via search] — [arXiv 2602.12026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12026); [arXiv 2604.01604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01604). An Aug 2026 preprint uses circuit tracing with concept-targeted attribution to study how linear probes emerge [V, title] — [arXiv 2608.27510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27510)
- Anthropic 2026: May 2026 Circuits Update ("Downstream Connections Predict Which Features Will Steer Model Behavior", Goel, Kauvar, Turner) uses CLT features and argues a feature's downstream connections, not its top activations/unembeds, predict its steering effect [V] — [May 2026 update](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/may-update/index.html). A 2026 transformer-circuits paper on interference weights in a tiny LM notes sparsely connected CLTs mask virtual weights during training and cites a replication finding that weight-sparse circuits can be unfaithful [V, via search summary] — [Characterizing interference weights in a tiny language model](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/interference_effectiveness_helpfulness/index.html)
- Parameter decomposition: APD (Braun, Bushnaq, Heimersheim, Mendel, Sharkey; Apollo, arXiv 2501.14926, Jan 2025) decomposes weights into parameter components that are faithful (sum to weights), minimal (few active per input) and simple — [arXiv 2501.14926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14926). SPD (Bushnaq, Braun, Sharkey; Goodfire, arXiv 2506.20790, v1 June 2025, v2 Sept 2025) is more scalable and hyperparameter-robust than APD, avoids shrinkage, removes APD's hard-to-set top-k, recovers ground-truth mechanisms in toy models; code released [V] — [arXiv 2506.20790](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20790v2)
- Weight-sparse training as an alternative to post-hoc dictionaries: OpenAI's "Weight-sparse transformers have interpretable circuits" (Gao et al., Nov 2025) trains models with most weights zero so circuits are small by construction — [OpenAI: Understanding neural networks through sparse circuits](https://openai.com/index/understanding-neural-networks-through-sparse-circuits/)

### Inferences
- For a primer: SAEs answer "what is represented here", transcoders/CLTs answer "what computation connects representations", crosscoders answer "what differs across layers/models", parameter decomposition answers "what mechanisms are in the weights". The practical frontier in 2025-26 is CLT attribution graphs; parameter decomposition remains mostly toy/small-model scale.

## Status of the field's view

### Takeaway
In March 2025 GDM's mech-interp team publicly deprioritized fundamental SAE research after SAE-based probes lost to dense linear probes OOD; the field's 2025-26 consensus is that SAEs are not a general-purpose replacement for supervised methods but remain useful for unsupervised discovery, auditing, dataset debugging, model diffing, and as the substrate for circuit tracing—shown by GDM itself releasing Gemma Scope 2 later in 2025 with an explicit "auditing games" and "red-teaming SAEs" agenda.

### Cited Findings
- GDM post "Negative Results for Sparse Autoencoders On Downstream Tasks and Deprioritising SAE Research" (Smith, Rajamanoharan, Conmy, McDougall, Lieberum, Kramár, Shah, Nanda; 26 Mar 2025): harmful-intent probes trained on HarmBench vs Alpaca; dense residual probes reach ~0.999 AUROC OOD; single-latent SAE probes fail to fit train; k≈20 sparse SAE probes fit and generalize in-distribution but transfer worse OOD; probes on SAE reconstructions lose ~0.048 AUROC OOD (GemmaScope) / 0.039 (chat-finetuned) [V] — [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)
- Same post: "deprioritising fundamental SAE research for the moment"; SAEs are not useless but the field may be over-invested; remaining uses: exploratory debugging of mysterious failures (citing Marks et al.), dataset debugging, toolkit item; encouraging signs include Matryoshka SAEs; new directions: model diffing, model organisms of deception, interpreting thinking models [V] — [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)
- Auditing games: Anthropic's "Auditing Language Models for Hidden Objectives" (Marks et al., arXiv 2503.10965, March 2025) trained a model with a hidden reward-model-sycophancy objective; 3 of 4 blind teams found it, and teams with SAE access used features to locate the training documents describing RM biases; SAEs were one of several effective techniques (alongside behavioral attacks and training-data search) — [arXiv 2503.10965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10965)
- GS2 (Sept 2025) research agenda explicitly includes "Auditing games: can we use SAEs to verify whether models are reasoning faithfully, planning deceptively, or pursuing hidden goals?", "Red-teaming SAEs: Can we find downstream tasks where SAEs can be measured against simple baselines", and model-diffing ("Can SAEs detect the traces left by finetuning (Minder et al., 2025)?") [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf)
- Anthropic continued dictionary-based work through 2026 (CLT-based circuits updates), with a 2026 emphasis on causal/downstream characterization of features rather than activation-only labels [V] — [May 2026 update](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/may-update/index.html)
- Skeptical evidence persisted into 2025: non-canonical units (Leask et al.) and seed dependence (Paulo & Belrose) [V] — [arXiv 2502.04878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04878); [arXiv 2501.16615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16615). SAEBench found proxy metrics poorly predict practical usefulness — [arXiv 2503.09532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09532)

### Inferences
- The GDM "deprioritization" is narrower than often reported: it deprioritized fundamental SAE-architecture research, not SAE use. GDM's own Dec 2025/Jan 2026 Gemma Scope 2 release shows continued investment in SAEs as infrastructure.
- Probe-vs-SAE comparisons are covered elsewhere; the one-line summary is "for a known concept with labels, dense probes win; for unknown concepts, SAEs offer discovery."

### Gaps
- I did not find a formal Anthropic written rebuttal to the GDM post; Anthropic's response has been continued publication (circuit tracing, biology, auditing) rather than a direct reply.
- No 2026 survey of field sentiment was found.

## Public SAE suites and tooling

### Takeaway
Major open suites: Gemma Scope (Gemma 2, 2024, JumpReLU), Gemma Scope 2 (Gemma 3 270M-27B PT and IT, all layers, SAEs + skip-transcoders + crosscoders + CLTs), Llama Scope (Llama-3.1-8B), OpenAI's GPT-2 small TopK SAEs (GPT-4 SAEs not released), EleutherAI Llama/Pythia SAEs and transcoders, Goodfire Llama SAEs, all loadable via SAELens and browsable on Neuronpedia.

### Cited Findings
- Gemma Scope (Lieberum et al., GDM, arXiv 2408.05147, Aug 2024): JumpReLU SAEs on every layer and sublayer of Gemma 2 2B and 9B and selected layers of 27B; also transcoders [V] — [arXiv 2408.05147](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05147)
- Gemma Scope 2: paper dated 2025-09-16; JumpReLU SAEs and skip-transcoders on all layers and sub-layers (attention output pre-W_O, MLP output, post-MLP residual) of Gemma 3 270M, 1B, 4B, 12B, 27B, PT and IT; partial weakly causal crosscoders for each model; CLTs for 270M and 1B; widths 16k-1M; IT SAEs finetuned from PT on chat rollouts; select KL-finetuned; CC-BY-4.0; SAELens loadable; Neuronpedia recommended for browsing; contact Callum McDougall [V] — [Gemma Scope 2 Technical Paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/gemma-scope-2-helping-the-ai-safety-community-deepen-understanding-of-complex-language-model-behavior/Gemma_Scope_2_Technical_Paper.pdf); [HF google/gemma-scope-2-4b-it](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope-2-4b-it); [HF google/gemma-scope-2-27b-it](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope-2-27b-it).
