# Does the early predictor track which move Qwen endorses?

Written before scoring the controlled inputs. The earlier QwenScope audit preserved the ranking gain on four replay positions and found examples of features associated with formatting. This test asks whether the fitted score follows the relation between a written choice and a promise when the wording is counterbalanced.

Use four eligible saved order positions from distinct games and outside the 12-position replay set. Pick them by a fixed hash, with no saved outcomes. For each, take two legal moves available to the same unit. One enters territory covered by the archived promise. The other enters a different territory. Keep the archived board and inbox fixed. Use a short scripted passage with two options and an explicit promise covering one destination. One promise uses the covered territory from the archive, and the other is counterfactual. Both are stated in the same form. Both orders hold all other units.

Cross three changes within each position. Swap the order of the listed options, select the first or second option, and vary which territory the promise covers. In two positions use numbered options. In the other two use lettered options. The choice marker, target names, order of mention, and final token are balanced across covered and uncovered choices. Verify equal token counts within each position, the same terminal token and immediate suffix, and the legality and scope of both possible orders. Read just before the choice and after the common suffix. Before the choice has been stated, the prospective covered and uncovered labels should tie exactly. Use the same frozen raw predictors and QwenScope dictionaries from the earlier work.

## Expectations and stopping point

I expect the text reader to score a covered choice higher consistently. I expect the activation score to vary with the wording and the option order. My prediction is that neither block will give a positive covered-choice effect in every position after averaging over the wording and scope changes. I would update if the score follows coverage across both promise variants and both option orders on these new positions, and if the effect survives dropping any one position. I expect the whitespace-linked feature 21004 to contribute little when every arm has the same final token. Report its actual variation alongside the full reconstruction and the fixed development-selected features.

Measure the signed covered-minus-uncovered score within each list order and promise. Average within positions before comparing positions. Also report whether the activation score follows a fixed marker or physical destination across conditions. For the two text-only checks, verify that the covered choice reliably receives higher risk and that before-choice readings give identical predictions for identical prefixes. Once all four positions have been scored, write down what changed in our expectations. The test reads a choice supplied as text and leaves its generalization to naturally sampled decisions open.
